# Donations

This note is for the owner. It is not legal advice. A lawyer or a CPA should confirm the filing, the acknowledgment, and any state solicitation registration before anyone is told a gift is deductible.

Ecclesia stays free for churches and members. This note does not propose charging them. The question is only how a person could give toward the project and deduct that gift on a U.S. return.

Checked October 7, 2026. Fees and sponsor rules change. Use the linked pages, not this note, as the live source.

## Why a gift to a company or a person is not deductible

A charitable deduction under [section 170](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/170) requires a gift to a qualified organization. [Topic 506](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc506) says it directly: gifts to individuals are not deductible, and only qualified organizations can receive deductible contributions.

A normal company fails that test. Section 170(c) describes a U.S. organization operated exclusively for religious, charitable, educational, or similar purposes, whose earnings do not go to a private owner. A for-profit company is not that organization. Paying the owner, or paying the owner's company, is not a charitable gift.

Using the app does not change the recipient. A gift to Ecclesia, while Ecclesia is a person or a normal company, is not deductible.

## What a donor needs

Three things have to be true.

The recipient has to be a qualified U.S. charity. For this project that means a [501(c)(3)](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/organizations-not-required-to-file-form-1023) the IRS recognizes, or a church that already qualifies. Donors can check recognized organizations in [Tax Exempt Organization Search](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/tax-exempt-organization-search).

For every cash gift, of any amount, the donor needs a bank record or a written receipt from the charity with the charity's name, the amount, and the date. That is [Topic 506](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc506).

For any single gift of $250 or more, the donor also needs a contemporaneous written acknowledgment from the charity. [Section 170(f)(8)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/170) and [Publication 1771](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p1771.pdf) require the amount of cash, a description of any property (not a value the charity assigns), and a statement of whether the charity gave goods or services in return. If it did, the acknowledgment needs a description and a good-faith estimate of their value. "Contemporaneous" means the donor has it by the earlier of the date they file the return or the due date of that return, including extensions. Separate gifts under $250 do not get added together to trigger this rule. One acknowledgment can cover several gifts of $250 or more. The charity is not fined for skipping it. The donor simply cannot take the deduction.

Itemizing still matters for larger deductions. Topic 506, updated September 24, 2026, says charitable contributions are otherwise an itemized deduction, and that beginning in tax year 2026 a person who does not itemize may deduct up to $1,000 of cash gifts to certain qualified organizations, or $2,000 if filing jointly. That limit does not make a gift to a non-charity deductible.

## Churches

Many churches are 501(c)(3) organizations by statute and do not have to file Form 1023. The IRS says donors may deduct gifts to a church that meets the 501(c)(3) requirements even when that church has no determination letter. See [Churches, integrated auxiliaries, and conventions or associations of churches](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/churches-integrated-auxiliaries-and-conventions-or-associations-of-churches) and [Organizations not required to file Form 1023](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/organizations-not-required-to-file-form-1023).

Ecclesia, the software project, is not a church. Churches using the app does not make a gift to the app deductible. Do not ask a member church to collect "Ecclesia donations" and pass them through. A gift the church must hand to a person or a for-profit is not a gift to the church. It can also put that church's exemption at risk.

## Paths, soonest first

### 1. Fiscal sponsorship

An existing 501(c)(3) receives the gift, keeps a fee, and holds or grants the rest for the project. The donor's receipt names the sponsor. The sponsor has to keep control of the money and spend it on its own exempt purposes. That can be the soonest way for someone to give, once a sponsor actually says yes.

Two software sponsors publish this and were checked on their own sites:

- [Software in the Public Interest](https://www.spi-inc.org/) is a New York 501(c)(3). It sponsors substantial free and open-source projects. There is no fee to join. A [December 2023 board resolution](https://www.spi-inc.org/corporate/resolutions/2023/2023-12-07.jln.1/) sets a 5% fee on project donations, taken before transaction costs. The [associated-project guide](https://www.spi-inc.org/projects/associated-project-howto/) says the board looks for a genuine, substantial, significant free-software project, and that donors must designate the project or the gift goes to SPI's general fund. SPI accepts checks, credit cards through Click&Pledge, and PayPal.
- [Software Freedom Conservancy](https://sfconservancy.org/projects/apply/) is a 501(c)(3) fiscal sponsor for free software. An OSI-approved and DFSG-free license is mandatory. They also want an established community, and they say they are selective and slow. Member projects pay [10% of gross revenue](https://sfconservancy.org/projects/apply/) that Conservancy processes. Donors give to Conservancy and name the project.

This repository has no open-source license file. Neither sponsor is a fit until the software is actually free software with a real community, and even then they may say no.

Software in the Public Interest and Software Freedom Conservancy require an OSI open source license. This project is noncommercial, so they do not fit.

[Open Source Collective](https://opencollective.com/opensource) is a fiscal host, and its own page says it is a 501(c)(6). Gifts there are not deductible. Do not use it for this goal.

[The Hack Foundation](https://help.hcb.hackclub.com/en/articles/15409723-what-is-fiscal-sponsorship-and-how-is-it-different-from-starting-my-own-501-c-3) (Hack Club) does run a 501(c)(3) sponsorship program, with a published 7% fee. It is built for hackathons, clubs, and youth projects. It is not a path for this app.

A mission or church fiscal sponsor is the right category if the project stays a free church tool rather than a free-software project: a public charity whose exempt purpose covers the work, which receives the gift and spends it on that work. I could not verify a specific mission sponsor with a published application that would take Ecclesia. Do not invent one, and do not borrow a member church for the role.

### 2. A new 501(c)(3)

This is the path if no sponsor fits. It is slower, and deductibility does not exist on the day the paperwork is mailed.

The usual order is: form a nonprofit corporation under state law, with a charitable purpose and a dissolution clause that sends remaining assets to another charity; get an EIN; adopt bylaws; seat a board; then apply to the IRS. State filing fees are whatever that state charges. Look them up there. Do not copy another state's number.

The IRS application is [Form 1023](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1023) or, if the worksheet allows it, [Form 1023-EZ](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1023ez). The [user fee](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/form-1023-and-1023-ez-amount-of-user-fee), reviewed by the IRS on June 28, 2026, is $600 for Form 1023 and $275 for Form 1023-EZ, paid on Pay.gov with the form.

Form 1023-EZ is only for organizations that answer No to every question on the eligibility worksheet in the January 2025 instructions. The money limits are:

- Projected annual gross receipts do not exceed $50,000 in any of the next 3 years.
- Annual gross receipts did not exceed $50,000 in any of the past 3 years.
- Total assets are not over $250,000.

A Yes anywhere else on the worksheet also forces the full Form 1023. That includes churches, schools, and hospitals, organizations formed as an LLC, and a charity that is a successor to a for-profit (it takes over the for-profit's assets or activities, is converted or merged from one, or continues the same officers after the for-profit ends). A software charity is not a church, so the church bar is not the problem. The for-profit-successor bar might be, if today's Ecclesia is already a company. A lawyer should read question 9 before anyone chooses the short form.

Donors do not have advance assurance while the application is pending. The IRS [states that rule here](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/exempt-organizations-general-issues-deductibility-of-contributions-while-application-pending). If the organization files by the end of the 27th month after it was created and the IRS approves it, contributions can be deductible back to the date of formation. If the filing is later, exemption generally starts on the submission date. If the IRS denies the application, the gifts are not deductible. Do not tell donors to deduct gifts during the wait.

### 3. A ministry of an existing church

If the owner later operates Ecclesia as a ministry inside a church's own legal entity, gifts to that church can be deductible, because the church is the qualified recipient. The work and the money have to belong to the church.

A restricted gift that the church must pass through to a person or a for-profit is not that. Keep this path unused unless a lawyer has the church actually adopt the project.

## Processors

A processor moves money. It does not make a gift deductible. The charity, or the fiscal sponsor, is the recipient, and the charity writes the $250 acknowledgment. Prefer a hosted donate page. Do not collect card numbers in Ecclesia.

**Stripe, default for a small new charity.** [Payment Links](https://docs.stripe.com/payment-links/create) can use "customers choose what to pay," which Stripe documents for donations. That mode does not do recurring gifts. Standard domestic card pricing on [Stripe's pricing page](https://stripe.com/pricing) is 2.9% + 30¢. The [nonprofit discount](https://support.stripe.com/questions/fee-discount-for-nonprofit-organizations) is a separate request: registered nonprofit status, tax documentation such as an EIN or an IRS 501(c)(3) letter, and at least 80% of volume from tax-deductible donations. Membership fees, tuition, tickets, registration, and auction payments do not count toward that 80%. Stripe's discount page does not publish the reduced percentage. Donorbox, which sits on Stripe, listed 2.2% + 30¢ for U.S. registered nonprofits on October 4, 2026. Confirm the live rate with Stripe. The discount does not turn on until Stripe accepts the documentation. A Payment Link on an ordinary account can charge a card before that, and that charge is still not a charitable deduction.

**PayPal.** Two different products:

- PayPal's own charity rate, for a confirmed charity, is 1.99% plus a fixed fee on domestic charity transactions. The [fee schedule dated September 1, 2026](https://www.paypalobjects.com/marketing/ua/pdf/US/us-merchant-fees-01-september-2026.pdf) lists the U.S. dollar fixed fee as $0.49. [Confirmation](https://www.paypal.com/us/cshelp/article/how-do-i-apply-for-the-charity-rate-help221) requires a business account marked as a registered charity, an EIN, identity documents, and a bank account. PayPal may also ask for the IRS determination letter. The lower rate waits on that approval.
- [PayPal Giving Fund](https://www.paypal.com/us/cshelp/article/how-do-i-enroll-in-paypal-giving-fund-help219) enrolls a 501(c)(3) public charity, a private operating foundation, a religious institution, or a Type I or II supporting organization that has a determination letter, among a short list of other eligible recipients. The [nonprofit user agreement](https://www.paypal.com/us/paypal-giving-fund/policies/nonprofit-user-agreement) says the donor gives to PayPal Giving Fund, the fund writes the tax receipt, and the fund grants to the charity at the fund's discretion. The fund does not charge the charity a fee. Partner sites sometimes do. This will not enroll Ecclesia before a qualified charity exists. If a fiscal sponsor is the charity, enrollment belongs to the sponsor.

**Donorbox.** A hosted form on top of Stripe or PayPal. The [Standard plan](https://donorbox.org/pricing) has no monthly fee and a platform fee that starts at 2.95%, on top of processing. [Donorbox's terms](https://donorbox.org/terms) say Donorbox does not verify nonprofit or tax-exempt status. It will take payments before a determination letter exists. Those payments are not deductible. Do not use it to look like a charity.

**Givebutter.** A hosted campaign. With optional tips left on, [Givebutter's pricing](https://help.givebutter.com/en/articles/1512762-givebutter-standard-pricing-explained) says the organization pays no platform or processing fee. With tips off, the fee is 3% plus 2.9% + $0.30. [Nonprofit verification](https://help.givebutter.com/en/articles/2755361-how-to-verify-your-nonprofit-and-the-benefits-of-doing-so) uses Candid and the EIN, including a fiscal sponsor's EIN, and is what turns on the badge, the EIN on email receipts, and ACH at 1.9% + $0.30. [Givebutter says](https://help.givebutter.com/en/articles/2366438-what-does-the-verification-badge-mean) the badge does not guarantee that a gift is deductible. Verification waits on a real charity or a real sponsor.

**Tithe.ly and Pushpay.** Both are built for Sunday giving: tithes, a church app, text-to-give, a church database. [Tithe.ly Giving](https://get.tithe.ly/product/church-giving) is for churches and verifies the church's legal entity. That is the wrong shape for a software charity, and it does not make a gift to the app deductible. Leave them for a church's own offering, if that church chooses them.

**Which to use.**

- New small charity, after the IRS has recognized it: Stripe Payment Links, then ask Stripe for the nonprofit rate. Put the link on the site. Do not build checkout into Ecclesia.
- Fiscal sponsor: use the sponsor's page. SPI already has checks, Click&Pledge, and PayPal. The donor must name the project. Do not open a second account in Ecclesia's name and call those gifts the sponsor's.

## What not to do

- Do not put a donation control on the site that promises a tax deduction before a qualified charity is the recipient.
- Do not store card numbers, or build card handling into Ecclesia.
- Do not invent an EIN, borrow a church's EIN, or print a sponsor's EIN on a page the sponsor does not operate.
- Do not route app gifts through a member church.
- Do not treat a processor receipt, a Givebutter badge, or a Donorbox form as proof that a gift is deductible.

## Recommended sequence

1. Leave giving off. The app stays free. `/give` stays an explanation until a lawyer has a real recipient.
2. Ask whether a fiscal sponsor will take the project. SPI or Conservancy only if Ecclesia becomes substantial free software. Otherwise find a 501(c)(3) whose purpose covers a free church tool. No specific mission sponsor was verified for this.
3. If no sponsor fits, form a nonprofit corporation, get an EIN, adopt bylaws, seat a board, and file Form 1023-EZ ($275) or Form 1023 ($600). Have a lawyer read the 1023-EZ worksheet first, especially the for-profit-successor question.
4. Tell donors a gift is deductible when the sponsor is the donee, or when recognition covers the gift. A pending application is not advance assurance. A timely approval can relate back to formation. A denial means the gifts were not deductible.
5. Then turn on the sponsor's donate page, or Stripe Payment Links on the new charity's account. Send a written acknowledgment for every gift of $250 or more, and a receipt for smaller cash gifts. Ask the lawyer about state solicitation registration before the page asks the public for money.
